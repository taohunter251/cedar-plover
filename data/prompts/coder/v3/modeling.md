MODELING
- Y up, X width, +Z toward the camera of the reference photo: orient and arrange the object so a camera on +Z sees what the photo shows (the same visible side, the same layout of separate items, the same tilt when it is part of the object). When the photo looks down on a flat or low object (you see the top of a pan, plate, tray, ring, bracelet, hat or open box), tilt the whole object toward +Z about the X axis by roughly the photo's camera elevation, usually 20-45 degrees (a positive rotation.x turns the top toward +Z), so the eye-level front views show it as the photo does instead of edge-on. Keep stands, bases and supports that belong to the object; no floor, backdrop, lights or labels.
- The judge compares four front views with the photo: azimuth 30 and 330 degrees at eye level, from 15 degrees below and from 30 degrees above (camera distance 2.0, FOV 49.1 degrees, white #ffffff background, neutral lighting: a key light that follows the camera plus a soft environment that metals reflect). Your previews use exactly these views. Hidden sides only need to be plausible and clean.
- Text, logos and labels: copy them exactly as the photo shows them (the same letters even when misspelled, placement, size and colors) as flat surface detail; never correct, translate or invent text. Draw them as thin geometry (a flat shape or box per stroke or glyph, slightly in front of the surface) or as a DataTexture pixel map on a plane; there is no font, canvas or text geometry.
- Colors: take sRGB hex values from the photo's lit mid-tones. White or pale objects must stay readable on the white background: use their real off-white tone and keep edges and parts visible through shading, not pure #ffffff.
- Plan silently: object identity, part hierarchy and counts, proportions, shared dimensions. Build parts from the shared dimensions so they touch; nothing floats.
- Cylinder, Cone and Capsule run along Y; Torus, Plane, Circle and Ring lie in XY (horizontal: rotation.x = -Math.PI / 2); a wheel cylinder on a +Z-facing vehicle needs rotation.z = Math.PI / 2.
- Lathe: Vector2(radius >= 0, y) points revolved around Y; open vessels trace outer wall, rim, inner wall. Extrude: Shape in XY extruded along +Z by option `depth`; openings via shape.holes (no booleans exist). Tube: `new THREE.TubeGeometry(new THREE.CatmullRomCurve3(vector3Array), segments, radius, radialSegments)`. Rod from a to b: CylinderGeometry of height |b - a| at the midpoint, `quaternion.setFromUnitVectors(new THREE.Vector3(0, 1, 0), dir.normalize())`. Repeated identical parts: InstancedMesh. 32-64 radial segments for large round parts.
- Materials: MeshStandardMaterial with sRGB hex colors from the photo; plastic, wood, fabric metalness 0 roughness 0.3-1; metal metalness 0.8-0.9 roughness 0.2-0.35 (lower metalness looks like pale matte plastic in the judge's front views); glass and clear plastic MeshPhysicalMaterial with a visible tint, transparent true and opacity at least 0.35, so its shape and edges stay visible. Thin or open surfaces: side THREE.DoubleSide. Lines are 1 px wide, so visible wires are thin tubes. Textures only for visible patterns: `new THREE.DataTexture(rgbaUint8Array, size, size, THREE.RGBAFormat)`, size <= 512, RepeatWrapping, colorSpace SRGBColorSpace for color maps and NoColorSpace for data maps, needsUpdate = true.

JUDGING
- The judge compares the four front views with the photo first. It also looks at the object from all around on a gray background under studio light, checks a list of the photo's features (parts, counts, colors, text, patterns) and rejects objects that look broken from any side. Build a complete, closed, plausible object from every side: no hollow backs, paper-thin cards or floating pieces.
- Keep the side facing +Z and the viewing angle that the photo shows: a flat or low object seen from above is tilted toward +Z as described above; face-on objects (signs, frames, screens, cards) present their face to +Z.

APPEARANCE
- Color is what the judge most often holds against a model: match each part's hue and lightness to the photo (beige stays beige, not gray; gold stays gold; cream is not white), and keep colors as saturated as in the photo: never lighten, grey out or pastelise; vivid stays vivid. Whites at most #e6e6e6 so they stay readable on the white background, blacks #1c1c1c to #2a2a2a so shading still shows.
- Metal must read as metal: silver, chrome and steel use a light base color (#c8c8c8 to #e0e0e0) with metalness 0.8-0.9 and roughness 0.2-0.35; gold, brass and copper keep their hue at the same metalness. On the judge's white front views low metalness turns them into pale matte plastic; only dark metal (gunmetal, black anodized) uses a dark base color.
